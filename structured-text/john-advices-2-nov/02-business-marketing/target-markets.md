# Target Market Strategy

## Core Principle: Choose Your Battles

### Companies to TARGET ✓

#### 1. Innovative Companies (PRIMARY)
**Characteristics:**
- Forward-thinking
- Willing to experiment
- Value speed over perfection
- Understand competitive advantage
- Not risk-averse

**Why Target Them:**
> "In the innovative segment, that is where you should be operating. Because those companies are not risk-averse. They want to improve."

**Value Proposition:**
- "We can O.P. you" (Over-Power)
- Teach how to use AI effectively
- Keep them up to date with latest tools
- Provide tools to enhance their competitive position

#### 2. Companies with "Fear of Being Left Out"
**Psychology:**
- Worried about falling behind competitors
- See AI as critical to survival
- May not fully understand AI but know they need it
- Willing to invest to stay relevant

**Note:**
> "You have the fear of being left out. A lot of companies are targeting that fear. Even if they have shitty products."

**Our Advantage:** Provide real value, not just fear-mongering

#### 3. Progressive Large Companies
**Example:** The company Sander works for
- International presence
- Already doing progressive work
- May have AI teams but underutilizing them
- Open to external expertise

**Opportunity:**
- Supplement existing AI initiatives
- Provide practical implementation
- Training and upskilling services

### Companies to AVOID ✗

#### 1. Conservative, Risk-Averse Companies
**Characteristics:**
- "We've always done it this way"
- Need extensive proof before any change
- Long decision-making processes
- Scared of new technology

**Why Avoid:**
> "AI is changed, blah blah blah. They are risk-averse, not your target group. Let them die with the dinosaurs."

**Reality:**
- They have money but won't spend it on innovation
- Will waste your time in endless evaluation
- Even if you win, implementation will be painful

#### 2. Companies Led by Conservative IT Departments
**The Problem:**
> "You have no clue how conservative IT departments normally are. They are so risk-averse, you have no clue."

**Why IT Departments Block Innovation:**
- Protecting their existing role
- Fear of job displacement
- Comfortable with status quo
- See external solutions as threats

**The Solution:**
- Don't sell to IT departments
- Sell to management/business leaders
- Position as business solution, not IT project

## Pricing Strategy

### Core Principle
> "You're not working for free."

### Pricing Models

#### 1. Per-Call / Usage-Based
**Best For:** Services with variable usage
- Customer feedback analysis
- API calls
- Document processing

**Structure:**
- First X calls free (trial)
- Then per-call pricing
- Volume discounts at tiers

#### 2. Subscription
**Best For:** Ongoing services
- Virtual assistant
- Monitoring services
- Regular workflows

**Structure:**
- €200-300/month per service (suggested)
- Different tiers based on volume/features
- Annual discount option

#### 3. Setup + Maintenance
**Best For:** Custom implementations
- N8N workflow setup
- System integration
- Custom automation

**Structure:**
- One-time setup fee
- Monthly/annual maintenance
- Additional changes separately priced

### Setting Price Points

**Considerations:**
1. Old-fashioned estimate × your efficiency = your pricing floor
2. Value to customer (what it replaces) = pricing ceiling
3. Your costs + desired margin = minimum viable price
4. Market rate research = competitive positioning

**Example Thinking:**
> "You can ask a high price, but hey, we are working fast. Time is more important for a lot of companies than the price."

## Sales Approach

### The Prototype Strategy

#### What to Offer for Free
**Prototype Only:**
- Quick proof of concept
- Shows what's possible
- Gets foot in door
- Builds trust

**Clear Boundaries:**
> "Prototype is free. Project is different. A project costs this amount."

#### Negotiation Timing
> "You should negotiate in advance and not in the end."

**Before Starting:**
- Scope definition
- Price agreement
- Timeline expectations
- Deliverables clarity

**Problem with Current Client:**
- Kept adding requirements
- No discussion of additional costs
- Scope creep without pricing adjustment

**Lesson:** Set boundaries early

### The Research-First Approach

**Before Meeting with Prospect:**
1. Deep company research (automated)
2. Identify key pain points
3. Research decision makers
4. Understand their competitors
5. Prepare customized pitch

**During Meeting:**
- Meeting assistant running
- Live transcription
- Context from previous meetings
- Real-time suggestions

> "You're so prepared that nobody ever was."

### Handling Competition

**When Customer Says:** "We can make that also"

**Response:**
> "Yes, you can make it also. That's going to take you this amount of time, it's going to take you this and this and this, you're going to have this and this and this type of problems. We have them also. And in the meantime, it costs you exactly the same amount."

**Translation:** "Yes, you could. But should you?"
